# Theory of Change

## Project

**Simulated Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) Flood Response**

This Theory of Change was developed for a simulated humanitarian response to severe flooding affecting vulnerable households in coastal Côte d'Ivoire.

The exercise follows the MEAL DPro approach and is designed to demonstrate how a humanitarian intervention can be translated into a clear causal pathway, with preconditions, assumptions and MEAL measures used to test the logic.

## Purpose

The Theory of Change explains **how and why the intervention is expected to contribute to improved food security and the ability of vulnerable flood-affected households to meet essential needs during the flood season.**

A key purpose of the exercise was to avoid assuming that cash assistance automatically leads to improved food security.

Instead, the pathway considers the intermediate changes and external conditions that need to hold.

## Main Causal Pathway

The central pathway developed for the project is:

**Flooding disrupts household livelihoods and economic resources**

↓

**Vulnerable flood-affected households are identified and verified**

↓

**A functioning mechanism exists to deliver the planned cash assistance**

↓

**Eligible households receive the intended cash assistance**

↓

**Households can access and use the cash**

↓

**Households have additional financial resources and increased purchasing power**

↓

**Households are better able to purchase available priority food and essential goods**

↓

**Households are better able to meet their priority food needs**

↓

**Households experience improved food security and improved ability to meet essential needs**

## Key Preconditions

The pathway depends on several important preconditions, including:

- Eligible households are identified and verified.
- A functioning cash-transfer mechanism is available.
- Cash can be delivered safely.
- Markets remain functional and physically accessible.
- Essential food and priority goods remain sufficiently available.

## Critical Assumptions

The Theory of Change also identifies conditions that are outside the direct control of the project and need to be monitored.

These include:

- Households are able to access and use the assistance.
- Households allocate a meaningful portion of the assistance to priority needs.
- Markets remain accessible and functional.
- Essential goods remain available.
- Prices do not increase enough to substantially erode the purchasing power of the assistance.
- No major additional shock overwhelms the effect of the intervention.

## How MEAL Tests the Theory of Change

The Theory of Change is not treated as a fixed diagram.

The MEAL system is designed to generate evidence that can test whether the expected pathway is occurring.

Examples include:

- Transfer monitoring
- Post-distribution monitoring
- Market monitoring
- Economic capacity measurement using an ECMEN-based module
- Food Consumption Score (FCS)
- Reduced Coping Strategies Index (rCSI)
- Baseline and follow-up comparison

These measures help assess both programme delivery and changes in household economic capacity, food consumption and food-related coping.

## Key Learning

One of the main lessons from developing this Theory of Change was that:

> **Cash assistance does not automatically lead to improved food security.**

Cash can increase purchasing power, but the pathway also depends on factors such as market accessibility, availability of essential goods and price stability.

This required separating:

- What the project directly delivers
- What changes are expected to occur
- What conditions need to be in place
- What assumptions need to be tested

This distinction became important for the subsequent Results Framework, Logframe, PMP and data-collection design.

## Portfolio Note

This is a **simulated humanitarian MEAL project created for learning and professional skills demonstration**.

The scenario, programme logic and subsequent datasets are fictional and should not be interpreted as real humanitarian field evidence or programme results.
