# Results Framework

## Project

**Simulated Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) Flood Response**

This Results Framework was developed for a simulated humanitarian response to severe flooding affecting vulnerable households in coastal Côte d'Ivoire.

It translates the causal logic established in the Theory of Change into a structured hierarchy of intended project results.

The framework follows the **MEAL DPro Guide** as the primary methodological reference.

## Purpose

The Results Framework helps define:

- What the project ultimately aims to contribute to
- The strategic objectives that guide the intervention
- The intermediate results expected from the intervention
- The outputs that the project is responsible for delivering

The framework therefore provides the foundation for selecting indicators and developing the project's MEAL Plan.

## Results Hierarchy

The framework uses four levels:

**Goal → Strategic Objectives → Intermediate Results → Outputs**

Activities are not included in the Results Framework. They are added at the Logframe level.

## Goal

**Vulnerable flood-affected households have improved food security and ability to meet essential needs during the flood season.**

## Strategic Objectives

### SO1

**Households have increased economic capacity to access essential food and basic goods.**

This objective focuses on the household's economic capacity and purchasing power, recognizing that cash assistance is expected to strengthen the resources available to households.

### SO2

**Households have improved food security and are better able to meet their food needs during the flood season.**

This objective focuses on the higher-level change in food security, food consumption and food-related coping.

## Intermediate Results

### IR1

**Eligible flood-affected households receive the planned cash assistance in full and on time.**

### IR2

**Assisted households are able to access the cash transfer through a functional transfer mechanism.**

These results address different dimensions of cash-transfer performance.

Receiving the planned transfer and successfully accessing the transfer are related but distinct. They are therefore treated as complementary results rather than as a strict sequential chain.

## Outputs

### Output 1

**Eligible flood-affected households are identified and verified for MPCA assistance.**

### Output 2

**Verified eligible households are processed for cash assistance through the selected transfer mechanism.**

### Output 3

**Households receive information on how to access and use the cash transfer and how to provide feedback or complaints.**

The outputs describe what the project directly delivers or accomplishes.

## Results Logic

The overall logic can be summarized as:

**Outputs**

Identification and verification  
+  
Processing of eligible households for cash assistance  
+  
Information and feedback mechanisms

↓

**Intermediate Results**

Eligible households receive planned cash assistance  
+  
Assisted households can access the cash transfer

↓

**Strategic Objectives**

Increased economic capacity to access essential food and basic goods  
↓  
Improved food security and ability to meet food needs

↓

**Goal**

Improved food security and ability to meet essential needs during the flood season.

This is a results hierarchy rather than a rigid deterministic sequence. Several results can contribute to higher-level results simultaneously, while external conditions also influence whether change occurs.

## Key Design Considerations

### Output vs Intermediate Result

A key distinction during the development of the framework was separating what the project **delivers** from the **change expected to occur**.

For example:

**Output:**  
Verified eligible households are processed for cash assistance through the selected transfer mechanism.

**Intermediate Result:**  
Eligible flood-affected households receive the planned cash assistance in full and on time.

The first describes a project deliverable. The second describes a change experienced at household level.

### Economic Capacity vs Food Security

The framework deliberately separates:

**SO1: Economic capacity**

from

**SO2: Food security**

Increased purchasing power does not automatically mean improved food security. Household food security can also be influenced by market access, availability of essential goods, prices and other contextual factors.

### External Conditions

Important external conditions identified in the Theory of Change, such as market functionality, availability of essential goods and price stability, are not treated as project results because they are not fully controlled by the project.

They remain important assumptions and contextual factors to monitor.

## Link to the Theory of Change

The Results Framework is derived from the project's Theory of Change.

The Theory of Change explains **why and how change is expected to happen**, including preconditions and assumptions.

The Results Framework then translates this broader causal logic into a structured set of results that can be monitored.

**Theory of Change**

Why and how change is expected

↓

**Results Framework**

What results the project intends to achieve

↓

**Logframe**

Results + activities + indicators + measurement methods + assumptions

↓

**PMP / MEAL Plan**

How those results will actually be measured and used

## Key Learning

Developing the Results Framework highlighted the importance of not turning every important condition into a project result.

For example, market access and price stability are critical to the Theory of Change, but they are not directly controlled by the project.

Another important learning was that results can be complementary rather than strictly sequential. Receiving cash and being able to access cash are different dimensions of programme performance and should not automatically be treated as one leading directly to the other.

The framework therefore aims to maintain a clear distinction between:

- Project deliverables
- Household-level changes
- Higher-level outcomes
- External enabling conditions

## Portfolio Note

This is a **simulated humanitarian MEAL project created for learning and professional skills demonstration**.

The scenario, results framework and subsequent datasets are fictional and should not be interpreted as real humanitarian field evidence or programme results.
