# MPCA Flood Response | MEAL Decision Dashboard

> **From evidence to findings, decisions and action**

An interactive bilingual MEAL dashboard for a simulated Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) flood response in coastal Côte d'Ivoire.

The dashboard translates the project's MEAL evidence chain into a decision-oriented presentation layer, connecting situational context, household outcomes, operational delivery, findings, management actions and evidence traceability.

---

## 🌍 Situational Context

Flooding disrupted livelihoods and reduced the ability of vulnerable households to meet essential needs during the flood season.

The simulated response uses **Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA)** to support eligible flood-affected households.

The programme logic assumes that:

- eligible households can be identified and verified;
- planned cash assistance can be delivered through a functioning transfer mechanism;
- households can access and use the assistance;
- markets remain sufficiently accessible and functional;
- essential goods remain available;
- prices remain sufficiently stable.

The dashboard therefore does not treat cash assistance as an automatic causal explanation for changes in household outcomes.

Instead, it follows the evidence from **baseline to follow-up**, identifies what changed, interprets the evidence cautiously, and connects findings to management action.

---

## 🎯 Dashboard Purpose

The dashboard is designed to answer four practical MEAL questions:

### 1. What happened?

What changed between baseline and follow-up?

### 2. What does the evidence show?

What do the household outcome and operational indicators tell us?

### 3. What does it mean?

What findings can reasonably be drawn from the available evidence?

### 4. What should happen next?

What management actions or evidence gaps require attention?

---

## 📊 Dashboard Scope

The dashboard brings together evidence on:

- Food Consumption Score (FCS)
- Reduced Coping Strategy Index (rCSI)
- Monthly per-capita economic capacity
- Full cash-transfer delivery
- Cash-access success
- Cash-access problems
- MPCA receipt
- Measurement gaps
- Findings and management actions

The analysis uses the same **60-household baseline/follow-up panel** where applicable.

For cash-access indicators, the denominator is explicitly protected:

**56 assisted households**, rather than the full 60-household panel.

## 📈 Interpretation Safeguards

### Descriptive comparison only

Baseline-to-follow-up comparisons describe observed change.

They do **not** establish that MPCA caused the observed changes.

### Denominator safeguards

Different indicators use different valid denominators.

For example:

- household panel indicators: **n = 60**
- cash-access indicators: **n = 56 assisted households**

### Economic-capacity adequacy

Economic capacity is reported as observed change.

The dashboard does **not** classify households as economically adequate or inadequate because no contextual MEB/SMEB threshold is available in the evidence base.

### Measurement gaps

Where planned indicators cannot be assessed, the dashboard keeps the gap visible rather than inventing a value.

---

## ⚠️ Key Evidence Caveats

The dashboard includes several important evidence limitations:

- delivery timeframe could not be assessed;
- targeting-verification performance could not be fully assessed;
- communication and feedback indicators were not available for follow-up assessment;
- contextual MEB/SMEB information is required before economic-capacity adequacy can be assessed.

These are presented as **measurement gaps**, not hidden assumptions.

---

## 🧭 Management Use

The findings are connected to planned management actions, including:

- reviewing FCS by community/vulnerability;
- disaggregating rCSI to identify higher-risk groups;
- validating the applicable MEB/SMEB;
- reconciling partial transfers and non-receipts;
- investigating cash-access problems;
- tracing eligible households that did not receive MPCA;
- restoring missing measurement fields and defining appropriate measurement timing.

These are **planned management responses**, not observed results.
