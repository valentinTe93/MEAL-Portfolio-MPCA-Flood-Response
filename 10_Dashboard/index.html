Situation & Response Context

Why the response was needed, what it aimed to change, and what the MEAL cycle examined

The situation

Flooding created an immediate household purchasing-power problem

During the flood season, flooding in coastal Côte d’Ivoire disrupted livelihoods and reduced vulnerable households’ ability to meet essential food and basic needs. The response therefore addressed an immediate need for purchasing power, while the programme logic also depended on functioning markets, available goods and sufficiently stable prices.

The response

Multipurpose Cash Assistance

The simulated response targeted 60 eligible flood-affected households and planned a 100,000 FCFA transfer through a cash transfer mechanism.

The MEAL lens

From delivery to household outcomes

The panel followed the same 60-household sample from baseline to follow-up to examine food consumption, food-related coping, economic capacity, transfer delivery and cash access.

Programme logic shown in this portfolio:
Flood shock→ Reduced purchasing power→ MPCA received→ Greater ability to purchase essentials→ Improved ability to meet food needs

Methodology & Evidence Notes

Interpretation safeguards that keep the dashboard evidence-led

Descriptive comparison

Baseline → follow-up results are presented as descriptive before/after evidence. They do not establish that MPCA caused the observed changes.

Indicator methods

FCS and rCSI use their defined recall periods and scoring methods. Economic capacity follows the documented ECMEN approach.

Denominators matter

Cash-access indicators use the 56 assisted households denominator. Transfer-delivery indicators use the 60 eligible-household panel denominator.

Evidence boundary: Economic-capacity adequacy remains TBD because no contextual MEB/SMEB threshold is available. Planned actions are management responses, not observed outcomes. Measurement gaps remain visible rather than being filled with assumptions.
