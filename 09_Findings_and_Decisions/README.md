# Stage 09: Findings, Decisions and Actions

## Purpose

Stage 09 moves the MEAL process from indicator results to interpretation, management decisions and concrete follow-up actions.

The findings are based on the validated baseline and follow-up datasets, the before-and-after comparison, and the updated IPTT from Stage 08.

This project uses simulated data for learning and portfolio purposes.

## Stage 09 workflow

**Validated data → Indicator results → Findings → Decisions → Actions → Follow-up evidence**

The purpose is not simply to report numbers, but to use evidence to identify what requires attention, what should be maintained, and what action should follow.

## Key findings

### F01. Food consumption improved

Acceptable FCS increased from **86.67% at baseline to 93.33% at follow-up**, an increase of **6.67 percentage points**.

**Decision:** Maintain outcome monitoring and examine whether improvement is consistent across communities and relevant vulnerability groups.

### F02. Food-related coping decreased

Mean rCSI decreased from **10.47 to 8.15**, a change of **-2.32 points**.

Lower rCSI indicates less reported food-related coping.

**Decision:** Continue monitoring coping and identify communities or household groups where elevated coping remains.

### F03. Economic capacity increased, but adequacy remains unclassified

Mean monthly per-capita economic capacity increased from approximately **58,731 FCFA to 68,290 FCFA**, an increase of approximately **9,559 FCFA**.

An applicable contextual **MEB/SMEB threshold was not established** for this simulated project.

**Decision:** Do not classify households as economically adequate until the applicable contextual threshold is established and validated.

### F04. Full transfer delivery requires attention

**83.33%** of eligible households received the planned transfer in full.

The follow-up dataset contained **50 full transfers, 6 partial transfers and 4 non-receipts** among 60 eligible households.

**Decision:** Investigate partial and non-receipt cases and reconcile them through payment records and programme follow-up.

### F05. Cash access was broadly successful, but barriers remained

Among the **56 assisted households**, **53 successfully accessed the cash**, representing **94.64%**. Three assisted households reported access problems, representing **5.36%**.

The denominator was corrected during Stage 08 so these indicators use assisted households, as required by the indicator definition.

**Decision:** Maintain the transfer mechanism while investigating and resolving reported access barriers.

### F06. Four eligible households did not receive any MPCA

The follow-up data identified **4 eligible households with no recorded MPCA receipt**.

**Decision:** The transfer cycle should not be considered fully reconciled until these cases have been traced and their status documented.

### F07. Measurement gaps affected some planned IPTT indicators

Some planned indicators could not be assessed at follow-up because the required variables or denominators were not captured in the locked follow-up questionnaire.

These included:

* delivery timeframe
* targeting verification follow-up
* communication and feedback indicators

**Decision:** Treat these as measurement gaps rather than inventing values.

## Decisions and actions

The Stage 09 workbook connects each finding to a management response.

Main action themes include:

* investigate partial and non-receipt cases
* review cash-access barriers
* disaggregate outcome indicators by relevant groups and locations
* establish and validate the applicable MEB/SMEB
* restore or define missing measurement variables and denominators
* maintain follow-up evidence for actions taken

## Learning note

### L01. Data should drive findings and decisions

The simulated follow-up outcomes were generated independently of IPTT targets.

Findings were then derived from validated results rather than creating data to produce predetermined conclusions.

**Data should drive findings and decisions, not the reverse.**

## Methodological safeguards

### Descriptive before-and-after analysis

The baseline and follow-up comparison uses the same 60 panel households.

The results are descriptive before-and-after observations. They do **not** establish causal attribution or prove that MPCA alone caused the observed changes.

### Denominator discipline

Indicator calculations use the denominator specified by the indicator definition.

For the cash-access indicators, the denominator is assisted households rather than all eligible households.

### No invented MEB/SMEB

The economic-capacity indicator cannot be classified as above or below an MEB/SMEB without a validated contextual threshold.

The value therefore remains **TBD**.

### Measurement gaps are documented

Where follow-up data did not support an IPTT calculation, the indicator remains unassessed.

This avoids presenting incomplete evidence as a valid result.

## Portfolio learning demonstrated

Stage 09 demonstrates the ability to:

* interpret MEAL indicators
* distinguish findings from raw numbers
* connect evidence to management decisions
* identify operational problems
* define concrete follow-up actions
* respect indicator denominators
* recognize measurement limitations
* avoid unsupported causal claims
* document learning from the MEAL process
