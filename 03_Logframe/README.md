# Logical Framework (Logframe)

## Project

**Simulated Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) Flood Response**

This Logical Framework was developed for a simulated humanitarian response to severe flooding affecting vulnerable households in coastal Côte d'Ivoire.

The Logframe builds on the Theory of Change and Results Framework developed earlier in the project and translates the results logic into a more operational planning structure.

The exercise follows the **MEAL DPro Guide** as the primary methodological reference.

## Purpose

The Logframe provides a structured overview of:

- What the project aims to achieve
- How results are organized
- How progress can be measured
- What activities are required to produce the outputs
- What assumptions may affect the results

It therefore connects the project's results logic with its operational activities and MEAL requirements.

## Results Hierarchy

The Logframe uses the following results hierarchy:

**Goal → Strategic Objectives → Intermediate Results → Outputs → Activities**

This builds on the four-level Results Framework by adding activities.

## Goal

**Vulnerable flood-affected households have improved food security and ability to meet essential needs during the flood season.**

## Strategic Objectives

### SO1
**Households have increased economic capacity to access essential food and basic goods.**

### SO2
**Households have improved food security and are better able to meet their food needs during the flood season.**

## Intermediate Results

### IR1
**Eligible flood-affected households receive the planned cash assistance in full and on time.**

### IR2
**Assisted households are able to access the cash transfer through a functional transfer mechanism.**

## Outputs

### Output 1
**Eligible flood-affected households are identified and verified for MPCA assistance.**

### Output 2
**Verified eligible households are processed for cash assistance through the selected transfer mechanism.**

### Output 3
**Households receive information on how to access and use the cash transfer and how to provide feedback or complaints.**

## Activities

The Logframe adds the activities required to deliver the outputs.

### Output 1 Activities

- Conduct community outreach, registration and initial identification of potentially eligible flood-affected households.
- Verify household eligibility against approved programme criteria and resolve required verification issues.
- Finalize and quality-check the verified targeting list for MPCA assistance.

### Output 2 Activities

- Coordinate with the selected payment/transfer provider and prepare verified payment instructions.
- Process planned cash transfers to verified eligible households.
- Reconcile payment records and follow up on failed, partial or discrepant transactions.

### Output 3 Activities

- Prepare clear information materials and key messages on transfer access, appropriate use and feedback/complaints channels.
- Disseminate information to assisted households through agreed channels.
- Explain and promote feedback and complaints channels and document issues requiring follow-up.

## Indicators and Measurement

The Logframe connects the results to indicators and measurement methods.

Examples include:

- Percentage of eligible households receiving the planned cash transfer in full
- Percentage of eligible households receiving the planned transfer within the programme-defined delivery timeframe
- Percentage of assisted households successfully accessing cash
- Percentage of assisted households reporting problems accessing cash
- Percentage of targeted households successfully identified and verified as eligible
- Percentage of planned cash transfers successfully processed
- Percentage of assisted households reporting receipt of relevant information
- Food Consumption Score (FCS)
- Reduced Coping Strategies Index (rCSI)
- ECMEN-based economic capacity indicator

These indicators are developed further in the project's PMP/MEAL Plan.

## Assumptions

The Logframe also identifies assumptions that complement the vertical results logic.

Important external conditions include:

- Markets remain functional and physically accessible.
- Essential food and priority goods remain sufficiently available.
- Prices remain sufficiently stable so that the value of assistance is not substantially eroded.
- Households are able to access and use the assistance.
- No major additional shock overwhelms the intervention.

These conditions are not treated as project results because they are not fully controlled by project management.

## Key Learning

One of the main lessons from developing the Logframe was the importance of keeping **activities, outputs and results distinct**.

For example:

**Activity:**  
Register and verify potentially eligible households.

**Output:**  
Eligible flood-affected households are identified and verified for MPCA assistance.

**Intermediate Result:**  
Eligible flood-affected households receive the planned cash assistance in full and on time.

**Strategic Objective:**  
Households have increased economic capacity to access essential food and basic goods.

Each level describes something different and therefore requires a different type of measurement.

Another important learning was that results do not always form a perfectly linear sequence. Some results are complementary rather than strictly sequential. For example, receiving a transfer and being able to access that transfer are related but distinct dimensions of programme performance.

## Relationship to Previous MEAL Tools

The Logframe builds on the previous stages of the project:

**Theory of Change**  
↓  
Explains why change is expected to happen.

**Results Framework**  
↓  
Structures the intended results.

**Logframe**  
↓  
Adds activities, indicators, measurement methods and assumptions.

**PMP / MEAL Plan**  
↓  
Defines how the indicators will actually be measured and used.

## Portfolio Note

This is a **simulated humanitarian MEAL project created for learning and professional skills demonstration**.

The scenario, programme logic, activities, indicators and subsequent datasets are fictional and should not be interpreted as real humanitarian field evidence or programme results.
